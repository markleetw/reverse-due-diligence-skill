Consumer economics

Subscriptions promise a trusted, low-distraction experience while brand advertising pays for exposure. These revenue sources create a real product-management trade-off.

Enterprise and financing

Trust API provides B2B services. Pulse requires capital and introduces credit risk. Both need engineering, security and data resources that Sentry may also depend on.

Implication for the Head: Group growth does not prove that Sentry gains resources. The board may ask its new leader to defend a profitable core instead of expanding reach.

07Group growth versus Sentry growth

Group perspective

Improved consolidated margins support continuing interviews, not assuming that every product is healthy.

Sentry perspective

Average MAU declined from 14.6M to 13.8M while subscriptions grew. Paid conversion, cohort retention and ARPPU are needed to determine whether this represents healthy monetization.

Denominator check: Group growth and the growth of the product you will actually own are different metrics. Separate controllable KPIs from consolidated figures.

10Ownership and governance

In the fictional case, founders hold 21% and North Harbor Capital increased its stake to 12% during 2025, securing one board seat. This could affect capital priorities, but does not establish product-level control.

Upside hypothesis

A strategic investor could bring enterprise customers, disciplined capital allocation and B2B capabilities.

Risk hypothesis

A greater focus on EBITDA could raise the bar for long-term trust investments and free consumer features.

Evidence boundary: A board seat does not prove an actual roadmap veto. Verify this hypothesis with recent decisions.

16Interview questions

P0 · Interview cheat sheet

  1. What can this Head decide independently?
  2. How many dedicated Sentry engineers, PMs and designers are guaranteed?
  3. Who arbitrates Sentry versus Pulse platform priorities?
  4. Which three KPIs define success after 12 months?
  5. When did Product last reject short-term revenue to protect trust?

P0 · Must resolve before accepting

1. Which decisions require CEO, COO or CTO sign-off?
Why: an ownership claim is not a formal decision matrix. Red flag: only vague statements about collaboration.
2. How much PM, Design and Engineering capacity is dedicated to Sentry?
Why: group headcount is not usable capacity. Red flag: shared teams counted as guaranteed resources.
3. Who sets cross-unit data-platform priorities?
Why: platform allocation may be the actual bottleneck. Red flag: no escalation process.
4. Who defines the Head's first-year KPIs, and which inputs can the Head control?
Why: avoid accountability for outcomes controlled by another unit.
5. Give a concrete case where Product protected user trust over short-term revenue.
Why: the decision reveals who truly holds power.

P1 · Negotiation and expectations

6. What capital allocation gates changed after the 2024 downturn?
Ask for a project that was actually stopped.
7. Is the core KPI shifting from MAU to paid retention?
Clarify whether the mandate is growth, retention or monetization.
8. Who controls pricing and ad exposure limits?
Clarify monetization authority.
9. Can the Head reorganize teams or make net-new hires?
Check funded mandate versus aspirational JD language.
10. Is AI about staff efficiency or customer experience?
Avoid an unbounded new KPI.
11. Did North Harbor's board seat change investment criteria?
Test governance implications.
12. Why did the prior Head or interim owner leave the role?
Distinguish a growth hire from role dysfunction.

P2 · If time permits

  • 13. Which international consumer market merits new funding?
  • 14. Has Trust API improved Sentry itself?
  • 15. How is long-term trust damage from advertising measured?
  • 16. What decisions regularly create CEO–Product disagreement?

Verify independently instead of asking in interviews

Historical financials, ownership, staffing disclosures, public product pricing and formal board membership.

Corrections recorded

  • Removed: “Build the team” was incorrectly interpreted as eight approved new hires.
  • Downgraded: Anonymous complaints became interview signals, not verified organizational facts.
  • Reframed: Pulse's 27.3% revenue contribution no longer proxies capital exposure.

Still unknown

  • Sentry's dedicated engineering capacity.
  • Final approval rights for P&L, pricing, hiring and budgets.
  • Cohort retention and subscription unit economics.
  • Pulse loan vintages, losses and financing sources.
  • The actual pay package, equity and working expectations.
Decision principle: Unknowns are not defects to fill with guesses. Turn them into questions whose answers could change the career decision.
